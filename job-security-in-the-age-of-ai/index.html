Job security in the age of AI

November 4, 2026

My hours got cut in half. I’m sure they’ll be cut entirely within a month.

Every day I ask where can I best place myself to make myself useful, indispensible. It’s pretty apparent that AI is good at writing bug-free code and my team was very good at generating a lot of code. I was one of the only members on my team with a long history of building scalable web applications and I was glad to fill in the knowledge gap by taking an advisory role for my team.

I spent half my day reviewing the deluge of AI written code. I saw this as an opportunity to educate and shape the code base in a way that would be simple, understandable, maintainable. I was the go to person to consult when making a decision on large refactors or new services. I made a deliberate effort to educate junior members and Product by providing the justification on any of my decisions and suggestions. I imagined that this willingness to educate and wealth of personal insight is something that AI can never provide. At this moment, I sit at around 1000 unique and thoughtful guide-setting contributions while the next engineer sits at 300, mostly nits. The engineer after that is at 10. I can confidently say I am effectively the sole real reviewer on the team.

Four months ago a teammate liked my thoughtful reviews so much they created a manually ran AI reviewer trained on my review comments. I expressed my distaste for this as it was a false mimicry of my actual thought and preferences, there is no central vision that drive any of the comments made by the AI reviewer. I feel that it would only inspire a false sense of code quality confidence as well as weaken my real feedback. It also devalues one of my core contributions I bring to the team, something I thought AI couldn’t replace.

This felt like an attack on me as the sole reviewer and were missing the point of why I was educating them in the first place. I hoped that my team would one day have enough confidence to make their own contributions to the review process. This would require more effort on the team but the outcome would also be faster review cycles with the side-benefit of having a more capable engineering staff. Despite my pushback, my CEO, Product, my engineering peers were all too excited at the prospect of speeding up review cycles. They even joked about how this will put me out of a job. Shortly after the meeting only one person messaged me privately to tell me that this was fucked up.

I felt unheard and incredibly alone.

Two weeks ago a teammate introduced an automated AI reviewer with the intention of allowing code to be mergeable with only AI approval. This was offensive to me in multiple ways. It tears down possibly the last and possibly only gate where a human has to be in the loop. Tickets can now be started and finished entirely through AI, from product ideation to merging, to testing. With no more human feedback, there is no guide-setting, slop can be merged with no supervision. This is strictly a bar-lowering move, a way to short-cut through the review process which is now clearly being treated as an obstacle to bypass than to participate in. The AI reviewer profile was set to a generic profile while they tested feature.

Last week I was told my hours would be cut. I felt this coming as I saw my ‘replacement’ slowly being built before me. On my first day off, a teammate that I considered my friend silently switched the automated review profile from the generic reviewer to my reviewer profile. The profile has been trained on a years worth of my comments. I guess I should be grateful that they had the decency to do it behind my back but forgetting that its on a ledger that’s public and permanent.

Many people often say ‘AI took my job’ in a way that’s not immediately traceable to a specific event. Mass layoffs happen indiscriminately at organizations often times with the CEO announcing that nebulous productivity gains with AI is making up for the reduction in staff. AI models also are trained on the entire Earth’s library of stolen content, the anger of being a victim of theft is shared across your peers. In my case, the theft is targeted and the event is clear.

The guilt, burden, shame, and anger are mine alone.

AI took my job.
